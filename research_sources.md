# Research Sources and Fact Checks

These sources support the documentary’s core facts and framing. Legal filings are allegations unless a court finding is specifically identified.

## Primary and technical sources

1. **U.S. Department of Justice, September 6, 2018 — Park Jin Hyok complaint announcement**  
   https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/north-korean-regime-backed-programmer-charged-conspiracy-conduct-multiple-cyber-attacks-and  
   Supports DOJ’s allegation that the conspiracy compromised Bangladesh Bank’s network using spear-phishing, accessed terminals interfacing with SWIFT, and sent fraudulent messages directing the New York Fed to transfer funds.

2. **U.S. Department of Justice, February 17, 2021 — Three North Korean hackers indictment announcement**  
   https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/three-north-korean-military-hackers-indicted-wide-ranging-scheme-commit-cyberattacks-and  
   Supports later U.S. allegations involving three North Korean military hackers and the Bangladesh Bank theft.

3. **U.S. Department of Justice, 2021 indictment PDF**  
   https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2021/02/17/dprk_hacking_-_indictment_0_0.pdf  
   Supports the indictment’s description of the February 4, 2016 attempt: approximately $951 million attempted, approximately $81 million transferred to the Philippines, and approximately $20 million to Sri Lanka.

4. **BAE Systems Threat Research, “Two bytes to $951m”**  
   https://www.baesystems.com/en-uk/insight/two-bytes-to-951m  
   Supports technical discussion of custom malware believed by researchers to be linked to the heist, including interaction with SWIFT Alliance Access and concealment functionality. This is a researcher assessment, not a court finding.

5. **SWIFT Customer Security Programme**  
   https://www.swift.com/myswift/customer-security-programme-csp  
   Background on SWIFT’s security program and customer security controls. Check current wording before publication.

## Fact-check summary

- **Attempted amount:** About $951 million across 35 fraudulent payment instructions, as stated in the DOJ indictment.
- **Amount stolen:** About $81 million went to accounts in the Philippines.
- **Sri Lanka transfer:** An approximately $20 million instruction was stopped after a reported beneficiary spelling discrepancy prompted clarification. Attribute the detail to contemporaneous reporting.
- **SWIFT’s role:** A financial messaging network. Do not say it held or directly moved the funds.
- **Printer incident:** A reported visibility and response failure. It was not the payment mechanism or the sole cause of the heist.
- **Initial access:** DOJ alleged spear-phishing. Public reporting and technical analysis describe malware. Do not invent a specific email, employee, or exact infection moment.
- **Attribution:** U.S. prosecutors alleged North Korean government-linked involvement in 2018 and 2021 filings. Do not present this as a trial verdict resolving every operational detail.
- **Recovery:** Approximately $15 million was returned. Do not imply all recovered funds came through one route unless separately sourced.

## Source limitations

Public sources do not establish every detail of the attackers’ path through Bangladesh Bank’s network, each individual’s role, or the complete final disposition of every dollar. The script uses qualified language where the public record is incomplete.
