# Production Notes

## Project

- **Working title:** *The Bangladesh Bank Heist: The $81 Million That Vanished*
- **Format:** YouTube investigative technology documentary
- **Target runtime:** 12–13 minutes; edit target 12:45
- **Narration:** American English; professional, measured, suspenseful, clear
- **Narration pace:** Approximately 130–140 words per minute, with holds on maps, documents, and key figures

## Editorial approach

- Build suspense through the record of events and missing evidence, not invented characters or dialogue.
- Keep the distinction clear between SWIFT messaging and actual settlement or movement of funds.
- Explain the printer as a compromised visibility/monitoring path and delayed warning, not the transfer mechanism.
- Attribute claims about state involvement to U.S. legal filings and use “alleged” where appropriate.
- Use “approximately” for rounded totals and explain why attempted and stolen amounts differ.
- Avoid certainty about undocumented technical steps or the complete trace of the funds.

## Narration and performance

- Favor short, declarative sentences in the cold open and closing.
- Read technical definitions plainly; do not overemphasize acronyms.
- Pronounce “SWIFT” as the word “swift.”
- Keep the voice calm and precise. Let pauses, sound design, and evidence provide tension.
- Avoid melodrama, mock dialogue, and villain characterization.

## Visual and graphics guidance

- Use abstract diagrams for network access, SWIFT messages, bank routing, and layered controls.
- Mark recreated interfaces and routes as illustrative when needed.
- Do not display credentials, account numbers, real beneficiary details, or operational instructions.
- Avoid depicting a direct breach of the New York Fed or a hack of SWIFT’s core network.
- Use a restrained palette: dark neutral tones, warm paper highlights, and limited red for suspicious activity.
- Keep all on-screen text in American English and readable on mobile screens.
- Use archival material only with verified date/location and rights clearance.

## Sound design and music

- Open with printer mechanics and room tone; use silence after the printer stops.
- Build a restrained pulse under the investigation. Avoid exaggerated “hacking” sounds.
- Use subtle tonal shifts for payment routing and laundering sequences.
- Keep narration intelligible above music.
- Resolve the score gently; do not imply the recovery story is closed.

## Graphics and fact presentation

- Core figures: approximately $951 million attempted; approximately $81 million stolen; approximately $15 million later returned.
- Distinguish attempted instructions from completed transfers and eventual loss.
- The Sri Lanka instruction was about $20 million and was stopped after a reported spelling discrepancy prompted clarification.
- Present DOJ attribution in a timeline labeled “U.S. allegations / legal filings.”
- Avoid language suggesting a court adjudicated every claim in the documentary.

## Accessibility and delivery

- Deliver captions in SRT and review them against final voice recording.
- Keep captions in manageable reading units and clear of important on-screen figures.
- Provide a transcript and source list with the video where practical.
- Check the final mix on phone speakers and headphones.

## Final editorial review

- Confirm figures and attribution against `research_sources.md`.
- Review every map route and money-flow graphic for overclaiming.
- Check that illustrative reconstructions cannot be mistaken for archival evidence.
- Replace draft SRT timings with timings conformed to the final narration and edit.
